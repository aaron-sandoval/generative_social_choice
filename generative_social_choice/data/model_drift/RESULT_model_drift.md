Generated by `generative_social_choice/data/model_drift/measure_model_drift.py`. Differences are today minus 2025, on the 0-4 rating scale. Brackets are bootstrap 95% CIs. The *spread ratio* is Var(today - 2025) / Var(2025 - 2025'), where the denominator comes from cells which were rated in two different 2025 runs; a ratio of 1 means today's ratings vary around the 2025 ratings exactly as much as 2025 ratings varied among themselves.

## Interpretation and decisions

- No mean shift is detectable for any rating type or for generation quality. Today's ratings are noisier: about 1.5-2x the 2025-vs-2025 mean squared difference, and today's own repeat ratings differ more than repeated 2025 ratings did. Today's gpt-4o-mini calls were served by many more backend configurations (system fingerprints) than in 2025. R3's bias CI fails the ±0.05 check only because it is wide (it includes 0); its point estimate is +0.03.
- Generation (gpt-4o): today's outputs lie within the 2025 spread for every generator, but are less varied. At T=0, the 15 new runs' round-0 outputs are almost identical to each other, while the 15 outputs of 2025 differed about as much as T=1 outputs do (tables above). The new baseline's candidates are therefore typical of 2025's, with less run-to-run variation, which narrows its CIs slightly but should not move its mean.
- A larger effect than drift: the line endings of the prompts. The data are stored with LF line endings, but this Windows checkout (core.autocrlf) turns newlines inside CSV fields into CRLF. The old baseline runs were rated with CRLF prompts (their logged prompts contain escaped CRLF), while GenerativeCC's ratings are only reproduced with LF prompts (R2 pilot); CRLF prompts rate about 0.1 higher.
- Decisions (user, 2026-11-03): the new persistent-pool baseline runs use LF prompts, to match GenerativeCC, and the runs proceed without re-rating GenerativeCC.
